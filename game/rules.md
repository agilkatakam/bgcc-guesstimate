# How the game runs

## Running order

| Step | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Team forming on phones (captains invite by BITS email) | 10 | Teams form on the website |
| Briefing: read the rules; teams read the case file and the exhibit list | 10 | Teams lock |
| Investigation: open exhibits, read, submit | no limit | Submissions open; the organiser closes the round |
| Reveal (presenter) while judges finish | 15 | Round closed |
| Winner | 5 | Round closed |

## Rules to read aloud

1. On your phones you can all read the **case file** and the list of **twelve exhibits**: each one's title and a one-line description of what's in it.
2. **Opening exhibits.** You can open any exhibit on the website, at any time, as many as you like. Your count is the number of **different** exhibits anyone on your team has opened. Re-opening one, or opening it again on a teammate's phone, is free; an accidental open still counts. The website keeps the count. Choose carefully: read the descriptions before you open anything.
3. **Work only with your own team.** Don't discuss the case with other teams, show them your screen or tell them what an exhibit says. A team that shares, or uses what another team shared, is disqualified. After you submit, don't discuss the case with anyone outside your team until the reveal.
4. **Answering.** Each team may submit **once**, on the website. Answer all three questions. Be specific: names, places, dates, times, and the facts that prove each point. Quote the exact times, places and numbers from the exhibits; a point you assert without the fact that proves it doesn't count. Commit to one answer for each point ("most likely" is fine). A vague answer, or one that gets a main point wrong (who did it, who is dead, who helped), is wrong.
5. **Winning.** Of the correct answers, the one whose team opened the **fewest exhibits** wins. If two teams opened the same number, the one that **submitted first** wins, so speed matters too. Your count is the number of exhibits your team had opened when it submitted. If no answer is fully correct, the answer that gets the most of the case right wins (ties: fewer exhibits, then earlier).
6. **Time.** There is no time limit. Submissions stay open until the organiser closes the round, with a 10-minute warning first. Anything not submitted by then doesn't count.

## Organiser kit
- The projector on `/screen` and two judges on `/admin`.

## The three questions
1. What happened, and who was involved?
2. Why did it happen?
3. Where is the person responsible now headed, and how?

## Judging procedure
1. Judges open Admin → Submissions. Each entry shows **which** exhibits the team had opened when it submitted, how many, and the submission time (server time, to the second).
2. For each entry, first check the opened exhibits against the groups in the answer key: a claim can only be ticked if the team opened its groups. Then tick the claims the answer makes (any wording). Mark **Correct** only if all six are ticked and nothing contradicts them; otherwise **Not correct**, and note how many claims it made. Use the rulings in plot.md §5.
3. Judge in ranking order: fewest exhibits first, earliest submission first within a count. The first **Correct** in that order is the provisional winner.
4. Nobody can be correct with fewer than 4 exhibits, so a correct 4 can't be beaten by anything submitted after it. Once the provisional winner is a 4 and every entry ranked above it has been judged, the result is settled; announce it at the reveal, not mid-round.
5. Two judges: number entries by submission order; Judge A takes odd, Judge B even. Both judges must agree on the provisional winner's "Correct" and on every "Not correct" ranked above it.
6. If no entry is Correct, the entry with the most claims ticked wins; ties go to fewer exhibits, then the earlier submission.
